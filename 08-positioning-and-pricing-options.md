# 08 — What to change in the story and the pricing (options)

Written 2026-10-02 from the research in files 01–07. These are **options and recommendations, not decisions**. Nothing here is built or changed in the project.

## Main point
Confidence in an application comes from proof (users, numbers), not from a new story. So change how Talenfra is described and priced, and **test the change in real conversations with staffing firms** in the next four weeks. The product already supports every option below. Don't claim anything that is not built (no ATS, Google Sheets or Slack yet), and don't invent price levels: ask staffing owners what they would pay per role or per batch in the first 10–15 conversations.

## Decision 1 — What you lead with (positioning)

| Option | The pitch | Merits | Demerits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Auditable screening, compliance-led** | "Screening you can defend to your client, the candidate and a regulator." Every score shows the CV lines behind it; a recruiter approves the rubric; there is an audit trail. | Matches the law (EU AI Act hiring rules from 2 Dec 2027, Colorado from 1 Jan 2027; NYC, Illinois, California already apply). Answers "why not Bullhorn or LinkedIn?" because a keyword matcher inside an ATS cannot show its working. Uses your strongest built asset. | Staffing owners may care more about speed than compliance — must be tested. |
| **B. Reactivation first** | "Turn the CV database you already own into placements." One-time batch: they upload the pile or import it from Drive; you return a ranked list for their open roles. | Easiest to sell: no integration, measurable result (good candidates found per role), runs in days, gives first user numbers fast. | One-time work, so you must show how it becomes a recurring product. This is an untested idea. |
| **C. AI screening desk** | You deliver finished shortlists, not software (YC's "sell the completed work" idea). | Fits YC's 2026 "AI-native services" language. | Needs strong proof of software-like margins; more like an agency. |

**Recommendation:** lead with **A as the story and B as the first offer**. In the application: "Auditable AI screening for staffing firms. We start by reactivating the CV databases they already own."
**Scalability:** A builds a moat over time; B brings the first customers quickly.

## Decision 2 — Pricing

| Option | What it is | Merits | Demerits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Per-role subscription / usage price** | A monthly price per active role, with a cap on CVs | Looks like software; predictable; grows with usage. Your cost is about $0.09 per CV, so margins can be high. | Small firms may hesitate to commit before they see results |
| **2. Fixed price per shortlist or per reactivation batch** | Pay as you go | Small first purchase, good for pilots | Lumpy revenue until customers repeat |
| **3. Pay per hire** | A percentage of salary (Prism, a YC company, charges 15%) | Largest upside; fits YC's language | Revenue arrives months later; tying your fee to placements can bias scoring and hurt your fairness story; may count as recruitment-agency activity in some countries — **a lawyer must check** |
| **4. Keep the current build + retainer** | $1,500 POC, $5,500 build, $2,200/month | Easiest to start | Investors read it as a dev shop |

**Recommendation:** a ladder.
1. Free audit (you already designed it).
2. Paid pilot — your $1,500 POC works as this.
3. Standard plan: Option 1.
4. Pay-as-you-go entry: Option 2.
5. Dedicated per-client instance as an **enterprise add-on** for firms that need their own data location.
6. Per-hire pricing only as a later experiment, after legal advice.

**Scalability:** one product with a normal price grows without adding people; the dedicated instance stays an upsell, not the default.

## What to say in the application (draft direction, not final wording)
- One sentence: who (staffing firms), pain (screening time and defensibility), what (evidence-backed, auditable screening).
- Why you win: shows its working, human approves, regulation-ready, runs in the client's own instance if needed.
- First offer: reactivate your existing CV database.
- Business model: one product with a per-role price; dedicated instance as enterprise.
- Proof: numbers from the first pilots (hours saved, agreement with the recruiter's own shortlist).

## Cautions
- Interviewers notice a pivot made only for the form. A pivot that comes from what customers said is far stronger.
- Update the website to match whatever you choose, and remove promises the engine cannot keep (see `04-gap-list.md` #4).
- Per-hire pricing and any claim about regulation need lawyer review.
